# Possible City

## Product thesis

People struggle to act on the places around them because conventional planning artifacts are abstract, expert-led, and slow. Possible City turns overlooked urban spaces into legible, grounded alternative futures and pairs every vision with a first practical intervention.

## Public beta

- One composite Singapore field study designed as a rigorous, clearly labelled proof of concept.
- Three vision lenses for each place: ecological, communal, and contemplative.
- A visual transformation, concise rationale, feasibility signals, and intervention ladder for every vision.
- A one-time photo transformation flow using GPT Image 2, with browser-side resizing and metadata removal.
- Before/after comparison and local download without storing source or generated images.
- A device-local proposal form so visitors can frame the next place without collecting personal data.
- Share-ready project story and accessible responsive presentation.

## Audience

Residents, students, educators, designers, neighbourhood groups, and civic practitioners who need a more tangible way to begin conversations about place.

## Success criteria

1. A visitor understands the idea within the first viewport.
2. Switching lenses materially changes the proposed future, not merely its styling.
3. Every future names a credible first move, steward group, and cost band.
4. The experience works on mobile, keyboard, and touch.
5. Speculation is clearly labelled and never presented as an approved plan.

## Beta boundaries

This version does not claim official planning status, accept money, collect precise user location, store uploaded or generated images, or publish community submissions. Photo processing is transient and limited to JPEG, PNG, and WebP public-space photos. Anonymous one-way network hashes are retained briefly for abuse control, plus a monthly generation total for the shared budget. Persistent submissions, public maps, and institutional partnerships require a later privacy and governance design.

## Progression

The next layer adds structured submissions, moderation, image provenance, impact tracking, and neighbourhood deliberation. Later releases can connect climate, accessibility, heritage, and planning datasets to make feasibility assessments more rigorous.
